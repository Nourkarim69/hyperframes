# Writing episodes that kids (and the algorithm) love

## The episode formula (3–5 min long-form)

1. **Cold open, 0–20 s**: something unexpected happens within the first 15 seconds (a star falls, a door knocks). No slow world-building before the hook.
2. **The problem**: one clear want or worry a preschooler understands (lost, scared, left out, can't do something yet).
3. **Ask the wise one**: a mentor gives a riddle-like clue, not the answer.
4. **Try and fail, with a laugh**: the first try fails in a funny, physical way. The laugh is the turning point.
5. **The kind solution**: friendship, sharing, trying again, saying sorry, being brave. The lesson is _shown_ by the solution, then said once by the narrator at the end.
6. **Calm ending + teaser**: a cosy final image, then "Next time..." to pull viewers into the next episode (series retention).

Pacing rules: a new visual event (move, gesture, camera change, sparkle) at least every 5–8 s; no line longer than ~15 words; repeat a catchphrase once per episode (Hazel: "Oh prickles!", Ollie: "Hoo-hoo!"); a rhyme or tiny song in the climax.

## Shorts (cut from the episode)

- One scene (or two short ones) = one Short, 25–55 s. Pick scenes that **start with a surprise** and **end on a reaction**.
- The hook pill on top is a question or a shock in ≤ 7 words: "A STAR fell into Pip's garden!", "Why is the star glowing?!". Capitalise the key word.
- Every Short ends with the "Watch the FULL EPISODE" card, so Shorts feed the long video.
- Post the Shorts over the days after the episode (1 per day), each title ends with `#shorts`.

## YouTube rules for kids content (must follow)

- Mark every video **Made for Kids** (COPPA): comments are off and ads are not personalised. Do not ask kids to comment.
- No scary peril that lasts, no violence, no mean jokes, no brand names, no "smash the like button". Gentle calls to action only ("See you next time, friends!").
- YouTube demonetises _repetitious / mass-produced_ content. Every episode needs a new story, new situations and a real lesson; never re-cut the same scenes into new episodes, never change only the title.
- Recurring characters, a recurring theme tune and a consistent look are what build a channel: keep `cast.json` and the music fixed.
- Disclose synthetic voices if YouTube asks ("altered or synthetic content" is about realistic people; cartoon voices usually don't need it, but the description can say "Voices generated with AI").

## Series bible checklist for a new show

Name + tagline, the hero's want, 2–3 friends with one clear trait each and a catchphrase, a mentor, 4–5 recurring places, the bedtime-calm tone, and 10 episode ideas (one lesson each).
